Optimism and Highs

Thoughts

I’ve been observing the market for a while, and right now there seems to be a strong wave of optimism—largely driven by AI. Across industries, AI is acting as a catalyst, boosting sentiment, investment, and expectations.

Recently, I was discussing with a friend the scale and intensity of the bets being placed in this space. When I look at it through a game theory lens, it feels like every major player is operating under the assumption that their bet will ultimately win. That belief alone is enough to inflate valuations across the board.

However, the reality is more nuanced. AI models today are not dramatically differentiated in performance. The gaps exist, but they’re not wide enough to clearly signal a single dominant winner. This raises an important question: what if there isn’t just one winner?

And even if there is—can that winner sustain its lead?

Given the pace of research and iteration, any advantage may be short-lived. The industry is evolving too quickly for moats to remain unchallenged for long.

On a related note, most major AI players are heavily betting on data scale and compute (larger datasets, bigger GPUs, more training). But I can’t help but feel there’s a more efficient path waiting to be discovered. A company that figures out how to achieve similar or better results with significantly less data or compute could disrupt the current trajectory entirely.

We’ve already seen glimpses of this with players like DeepSeek making unexpected waves. It’s unlikely they’ll be the last. Innovation in efficiency—not just scale—might define the next phase.

Consequences

If raw data and massive compute aren’t the ultimate determinants of success, then it’s worth questioning current valuations—especially in sectors like semiconductors.

Are these prices justified by long-term fundamentals, or are they primarily being propped up by short-term demand-supply dynamics?

At the same time, we’re seeing intense competition among major AI labs—OpenAI, Anthropic, Google (Gemini), Alibaba (Qwen), and others—all racing to establish dominance. Alongside them, large tech companies like Amazon and Microsoft are making massive capital investments, effectively hedging across multiple potential winners.

There’s also a parallel surge of capital flowing into startups.

Having spent time around startup ecosystems, I’ve seen firsthand how much of this space runs on hype. Not all claims hold up under scrutiny. There’s a non-trivial amount of overstatement, narrative-building, and speculative positioning.

This makes the overall picture feel overheated.

To be clear, I’m not saying there’s no real impact—AI is already transformative. But the scale of the bets being placed feels disproportionate to the current level of certainty.

It raises broader questions:
- Are we inflating another cycle through debt and speculation?
- Is this simply the new normal?
- Or are we setting up for a correction?

I don’t have clear answers yet.

Action

Given this uncertainty, one practical takeaway for me is to prepare for downturn scenarios.

If a correction does come, the ability to make money—or at least preserve capital—during that phase becomes critical.

Learning how to operate in bearish or volatile markets might not just be useful—it could be necessary to survive the wave.
